ISSUES - MALARIA

Hawadak et al. 2026 (Delhi) - primary study, 9 ledger rows (MAL-001 to MAL-009)
1. Selection: patients needed undifferentiated fever >=3 days AND clinical suspicion of malaria. Fever-duration features (fever_prolonged) were not extracted; fever itself is an inclusion criterion.
2. Co-infection: 37/87 (42.5%) of malaria cases had dengue, chikungunya or typhoid co-infection. The authors state symptom proportions are probably overestimated for this reason.
3. Only 16 Pf cases, 70 Pv, 1 mixed. Mostly vivax. The Pf-vs-Pv symptom split in Fig 2B was not entered (one row per paper per feature).
4. 'Generalised body ache' (54.02% in text and Fig 2A; 47/87 if back-calculated) was NOT entered: the paper also reports 'Myalgia' (56.32%, 49/87 with counts), and the validator allows one row per paper per feature. Myalgia was kept because its count is printed. Table 1 body-ache row is also inconsistent (29 [51.8] + 27 [48.2] = 56, percentages computed on 56).
5. Table 1 Arthralgia row is identical to the Nausea/Vomiting row (10 [32.3], 13 [23.2], p=0.252). Possible copy error. Totals still match Fig 2A (23/87 each).
6. Severe-case percentages in the text (72.3%, 32.2%, 30.5%, 20.3%) fit a denominator of about 59, not the 56 severe cases reported. Not used.
7. Typos in the paper: abstract prevalence 2.04% vs conclusion 2.07% (87/4259 = 2.04%); 'hepatosplenomegaly (64.37)' missing % sign; haemoglobin '9.50.58'; male aOR 1.61 (CI 1.37-3.45) in text/Table 2 vs 1.42 in abstract.
8. Features reported but with no matching dictionary feature (not extracted): chills 70/87, headache 36/87, convulsions 13/87, retro-bulbar pain 9/87.
9. Possible overlap to check: same hospital and authors as Matlani 2020 (Ann Clin Microbiol Antimicrob 19:49) and Arya 2023 (J Trop Pediatr 69:fmad034). Study periods unknown; coordinator should check.
10. source_script_ref set to not_in_script because the teammate found this paper himself. Please confirm against the gen_synthetic_table script.
11. No PMID printed in the PDF; DOI used.

Karoli et al. 2021 (Lucknow) - primary study, 5 ledger rows (MAL-011 to MAL-015)
1. Cohort: 295 adult inpatients (>15 y, so 16-17 year olds are included; age_group set to adult), smear and/or RDT confirmed, Pv 183 / Pf 85 / mixed 27. Pregnant women and patients with positive serology for dengue, leptospirosis, scrub typhus, typhoid, HBV, HCV or HIV were excluded, so co-infection is much less than in Hawadak 2026. Tertiary referral hospital, many partially treated.
2. Table 4a (uncomplicated n=227 vs severe n=68) does not add up to the Table 1 totals. Weighted back from the 4a percentages: icterus ~93 (Table 1: 136), vomiting ~93 (134), abdominal pain ~69 (86), splenomegaly ~95 (102), headache ~223 (142). Table 1 totals were used because they match the Results text (icterus 46.2%, splenomegaly 34.6%, abdominal pain 29%).
3. Altered sensorium: Table 1 says 90 (30.4%), the Results text says 16%. Not a dictionary feature, so not extracted.
4. Vomiting: text says 46%, Table 1 says 134 (45.5%); 134/295 = 45.4%. Count recorded.
5. Table 5 (severe-only, Pv n=47, Pf n=21) has percentages that look inconsistent with those n (e.g. shock 10 and 21; Hb<5 43.30 vs 17). Not used; severe-only subset anyway.
6. Superficial bleeding (subconjunctival and gum) reported only as '2%' (rounded; no count). Not entered, because 2% x 295 is not a reliable k. hemorrhagic_signs component rows not created.
7. Not extracted (no dictionary feature): fever 295 (100%), chills 217, headache 142, pallor 180, convulsion 11, altered sensorium, shock 19, decreased urine output 58. No rash, arthralgia, myalgia, cough, lymphadenopathy or weight-loss data.
8. Typos in paper: 'Hb <5 mg/dl' (should be g/dl); 'Cerebrospinal fluid analysis was G6PD screening test'; 'cerebral malria'.
9. source_script_ref set to not_in_script (teammate found the paper). No PMID printed in the PDF; DOI used. No overlap with Hawadak 2026 (different hospital and city).

Shah et al. 2016 (Ahmedabad) - primary study, 6 ledger rows (MAL-016 to MAL-021)
1. Cohort: 51 indoor patients over 15 years (age_group set to adult; includes 16-17 year olds), smear and/or RDT confirmed, Pv 40 / Pf 8 / mixed 3, retrospective, Jun-Nov 2013, single centre. No severe malaria and no deaths in the cohort. 5 patients had a concurrent UTI; no serology exclusions stated.
2. Table 1 percentages match counts (13/51 = 25.49 etc.). Table 1 is marked 'multiple responses'.
3. Jaundice 3/51 and splenomegaly 10/51 come from the Results text only (no percentage printed, reported_pct = NA). The abstract says splenomegaly was the commonest presenting sign.
4. Bleeding: text says 'None of the patients suffered from any kind of bleeding diathesis' (p.34). Entered as hemorrhagic_signs 0/51, mapping proxy because the wording is 'bleeding diathesis'. Coordinator to confirm whether a stated zero is acceptable.
5. Internal inconsistency in the paper: abstract/discussion say moderate-to-severe anaemia in 24 patients (82.75%), but 24/51 = 47%. 82.75% fits 24/29 (males only). Not a dictionary feature, so not extracted.
6. Nausea (8/51), headache, giddiness, breathlessness (2/51), pallor (9) reported; no matching dictionary feature (nausea alone is not accepted as vomiting). No rash, arthralgia, myalgia or cough data.
7. No DOI or PMID is printed in the PDF. The source URL is in sources_malaria.csv.

Sharma et al. 2013 (New Delhi, children) - primary study, 3 ledger rows (MAL-022 to MAL-024), ALL poolable N
1. Cohort: 54 admitted children with P. vivax mono-infection (smear and/or RDT), retrospective record review, May-Oct 2011. P. falciparum, mixed infection, enteric fever, UTI, meningitis and OPD cases were excluded, and the authors say only 'sick' vivax cases were studied. This is a species-selected, admitted-only subset, not all confirmed malaria, so rows were set to poolable N. Coordinator to decide whether it can count as poolable.
2. Aim of the paper was to describe severe manifestations; the table lists only 'unusual manifestations', so splenomegaly (72.2%) and hepatomegaly (81.5%) are in the Results text only.
3. Same hospital (Safdarjung / VMMC, New Delhi) as Hawadak 2026. Hawadak's study period is not known; Sharma's is May-Oct 2011. Flagged possible_overlap_with MAL-001 in the notes.
4. Jaundice is printed as 'Jaundice' in the table and 'Icterus with deranged liver functions' in the text. Treated as clinical jaundice (not a lab-only definition); coordinator to confirm.
5. Convulsions: 4 (7.4%) in the table but 7.6% in the Results text and abstract. Not a dictionary feature, so not extracted. Table numbering is also repeated (two item 5s).
6. Nothing on vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, rash, arthralgia, myalgia or cough.
