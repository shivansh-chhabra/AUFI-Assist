ISSUES - TYPHOID

Chowta & Chowta 2005 (Mangalore) - primary study, 9 ledger rows (TYP-001 to TYP-009), ALL poolable N
1. Case definition is loose. Diagnosis was 'based on clinical features, widal test and blood culture'. Blood culture was positive in only 25% (11 of 44) and a single Widal with O titre >=1:160 in 88.6%. The paper does not say how many cases were confirmed by a defined test. A single Widal is non-specific. Rows are poolable N pending a coordinator decision; if he accepts the definition they can be changed to Y.
2. Old and small: 44 adult inpatients, 1999-2001, one teaching hospital. Mean age 23.9 years; 21 males, 23 females. Brief communication.
3. Splenomegaly (34.09%) appears only in the Results text, not in Table 1, and no count is given. k=15 is back-calculated (DERIVED, poolable N). Hepatomegaly 18% is also reported but is not a dictionary feature.
4. Jaundice (1) and bleeding per rectum (1) are listed under 'complications during hospitalisation' (Table 2), not under presenting symptoms. 'Bleeding per rectum' is recorded as a hemorrhagic_signs component named rectal_bleeding, which is not on the dictionary's component list; coordinator to confirm.
5. Co-infection or misdiagnosis is possible: malaria smear was positive in one patient and dengue antibody in another. No exclusion for either is stated.
6. Rash (including rose spots), fever pattern and duration, lymphadenopathy, conjunctival suffusion, arthralgia and night sweats are not reported.
7. Reported but with no dictionary feature (not extracted): fever 44 (100%), headache 8, constipation 4, breathlessness 1, burning micturition 1, hepatomegaly, bradycardia 10 (22.7%), myocarditis 2, one death from DIC.
8. source_script_ref set to not_in_script. No DOI or PMID is printed in the PDF; the journal and page range are given instead. Please confirm against the gen_synthetic_table script.

Kandikuppa et al. 2024 (Chennai) - primary study, 6 ledger rows (TYP-010 to TYP-015), poolable Y / C
1. Case definition is strong: blood culture (BACT/ALERT, MALDI-TOF, serotyping). But 57 of 125 (45.6%) had Salmonella paratyphi A, not S. typhi. Rows are marked Y/C because enteric fever is clinically near-identical and, per the data teammate's decision, paratyphoid is counted as typhoid (coordinator to confirm). Children (55) and adults (70) are mixed, so age_group is mixed; adults alone had 64% typhi.
2. Cohort is the 125 hospitalised patients out of 213 culture-proven cases (88 outpatients excluded), so it leans more severe than all culture-proven cases. No deaths, no intestinal hemorrhage or perforation. Retrospective, Oct 2018-Mar 2023, one referral hospital; 38.4% had antibiotics before admission and 79% came from Tamil Nadu.
3. 'Nausea or vomiting' is one printed item; recorded as vomiting proxy (component) per the dictionary.
4. Fever duration was given only in bands (<1 week 51, 1-3 weeks 69, >3 weeks 5), so the >14 days feature (fever_prolonged) cannot be extracted.
5. 'Intestinal hemorrhage or perforation' is 0 as one combined item. Not entered as a bleeding component because it is combined with perforation.
6. Rose spots (2) recorded as a rash proxy; the paper does not describe any other rash.
7. Table 4 'moderate hepatitis 7 (7.69)' uses 91 patients with transaminases as the denominator, not 125. Not extracted (no dictionary feature, and jaundice is not reported).
8. Reported but with no dictionary feature (not extracted): headache 29, constipation 3, fatigue or weakness 37, bradycardia 11 (8.8%), leukopenia 18, eosinopenia 102, thrombocytopenia 25.
9. Not reported: jaundice, weight loss, lymphadenopathy, conjunctival suffusion, arthralgia, myalgia, night sweats.
10. source_script_ref set to not_in_script. No PMID in the PDF; DOI used. Different hospital and period from Chowta 2005 (Mangalore, 1999-2001), so no overlap.

Kumar et al. 2012 (Manipal) - primary study, 6 ledger rows (TYP-016 to TYP-021)
1. Cohort: 106 culture-positive Salmonella typhi inpatients (of 616 clinically suspected) at a tertiary teaching hospital in Karnataka, 2005-2008, retrospective. Widal-only cases were excluded, so the case definition is good and S. typhi only. Mean age 28.8 years; 55.7% aged 15-30; 4 children (3.8%), so age_group is mixed. 78.3% male. Referral hospital catering to cases 'unsuccessfully treated elsewhere' (stated in the Introduction), so cases may be more severe than average.
2. Body ache: the Abstract says 'generalized body ache in 53 (32.5%)', but 53/106 = 50.0%. Body ache is not mentioned in the Results text or any table, so the number cannot be checked (53/163 would give 32.5%). Recorded as printed in TYP-020, poolable N (validator warning is this row).
3. Splenomegaly 47 (44.3%) is stated in the Results text. Hepatosplenomegaly (23, 21.7%) is reported separately and was not used for splenomegaly.
4. Gastrointestinal bleed (1) is a listed complication with no percentage; recorded as hemorrhagic_signs component 'gi_bleeding', which is not on the dictionary's component list, so coordinator should confirm. One patient each also had GI perforation, myocarditis and encephalitis (no dictionary features).
5. Raised liver enzymes in 49.1% is not jaundice (dictionary rejects abnormal LFT without clinical jaundice), so no jaundice row. Jaundice, rash, cough, weight loss, lymphadenopathy are not reported.
6. Reported but with no dictionary feature (not extracted): fever 106 (100%), headache 63, constipation 7.5%, hepatomegaly 42, abdominal tenderness 23, Widal positive 73 (68.9%).
7. Same medical college group as Chowta 2005 (Mangalore campus) but a different hospital and years (2005-2008 vs 1999-2001), so no overlap.
8. source_script_ref set to not_in_script. No PMID in the PDF; DOI used.

Gopal 2020 (Firozabad, Uttar Pradesh) - primary study, 4 ledger rows (TYP-022 to TYP-025), ALL poolable N
1. Case definition is unclear. 107 'cases of typhoid fever' were studied, but Table III shows blood culture positive in only 92 (85 Widal-positive, 7 Widal-negative) and negative in 15 (13 Widal-positive, 2 Widal-negative). So 15 patients (14%) were not culture-confirmed and 2 had neither a positive culture nor a positive Widal. The paper never states how a 'case' was defined or how patients were selected. Rows are poolable N pending a coordinator decision.
2. Study period, hospital name and setting (inpatient or outpatient) are not stated. Ages are only 20-60 years in Table I (65 males, 42 females, which sums to 107), so age_group is adult.
3. Fever is reported in only 102 of 107 patients (95%), so five 'typhoid' patients had no fever; this is not a dictionary feature but supports concern about the case definition.
4. Only counts are given (no percentages), so reported_pct is NA. The 'P value 0.01' for fever has no meaning and was ignored. The journal's quality was not assessed.
5. Reported but with no dictionary feature (not extracted): fever 102, headache 62, constipation 34, anorexia 5. Not reported: splenomegaly, rash or rose spots, jaundice, bleeding, weight loss, lymphadenopathy. The Discussion mentions myalgia and chills in general terms without counts.
6. source_script_ref set to not_in_script. No article DOI or PMID is printed in the PDF (only the journal's DOI prefix).

Pandit et al. 2012 (Manipal) - primary study, 6 ledger rows (TYP-026 to TYP-031)
1. This is a PARATYPHOID series (Salmonella paratyphi), not S. typhi. Decision from the data teammate: paratyphoid is counted as typhoid (enteric fever) in the 'Typhoid fever' cell, so splenomegaly, rash, vomiting, abdominal pain and GI bleeding are poolable Y/C. Coordinator to confirm. Diarrhoea stays poolable N because its count is back-calculated. 110 culture-positive cases of 141 reviewed, Kasturba Hospital, Manipal, Jan 2005-Dec 2010, retrospective; Widal-only cases were excluded. The case definition is good.
2. Possible overlap to rule out: same hospital, same group of authors and overlapping years as Kumar 2012 (S. typhi, 2005-2008, TYP-016 to TYP-021). The organisms differ (S. typhi vs S. paratyphi), so the patients cannot be the same and the cohorts were not double-counted.
3. Diarrhoea is printed only as '23.6%' in the Results with no count; k=26 is back-calculated (DERIVED, poolable N). Vomiting 35 (31.8%) and abdominal pain 15 (13.6%) are given as counts in the text.
4. Table 2 prints the aminoglycoside row as 76 (90.4%), but 76/110 = 69%; the antibiotic data are not used. The text also says '141 cases of paratyphoid fever, out of which 110 positive on blood culture', so the 110 are the culture-positive subset. 'Enteric hepatitis' 64 (58.1%) is raised liver enzymes, not jaundice, so no jaundice row.
5. Rash is 'rash' (any); rose spots are not mentioned. Cough, jaundice, weight loss, fever pattern and duration, lymphadenopathy are not reported. Hepatomegaly (34) and hepatosplenomegaly (16) have no matching dictionary feature (or are combined).
6. source_script_ref set to not_in_script. No PMID in the PDF; DOI used.
